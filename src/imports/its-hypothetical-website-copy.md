# It's Hypothetical — Website Copy & Structure
For Framer build. Multi-page: homepage + one page per project.

Voice across the whole site: warm, deadpan, confident, short sentences, two-beat contrast lines. No exclamation marks unless something's actually urgent. Never lecture, never oversell.

**Personal voice (About/hero, first-person moments) — pulled from your cover letters and interview answers:**
Humble but a bit hehe. The pattern in your own writing: state something confident, then undercut it with something small, specific, and slightly self-deprecating — never a caveat that weakens the point, just one that makes it human. ("I'm kind of funny." "Still standing, mostly." "Don't hate me, I'm old, and this is very live-laugh-love, but I do think empathy is one of my strengths.") It's not self-doubt — it's confidence that doesn't need to perform. Avoid corporate polish and avoid grand claims; ground everything in one concrete, specific detail rather than an abstract one. This voice is distinct from the product voices below — Loud & Fine and Quirks & All are deadpan *about the dog*, this is deadpan *about yourself*.

## PROJECT PAGE TEMPLATE
(Structure below applies to every project page — filled in per-project after)

1. **Back link** — `← All projects`
2. **Hero** — App name, tagline, one-sentence positioning, primary CTA (App Store badge / waitlist / "Get notified")
3. **The problem** — 2-3 sentences on what's actually broken, no jargon
4. **How it works** — 3-4 short beats, not a feature list, more "here's the actual experience"
5. **Voice sample / product moment** — a real screen line or two, shown as a quote or mock screen, to prove the tone isn't just marketing talk
6. **Details strip** — price, platform, one-time vs subscription (people appreciate this being upfront)
7. **CTA repeat** — same as hero
8. **Footer** — link back to home + next project

## LOUD & FINE — /loud-and-fine

**Hero**
H1: Loud & Fine
Tagline: *Loud world. Fine dog.*
Sub: Systematic sound desensitization for dogs who lose it at vacuums, thunder, fireworks, and the doorbell. $2.99 once. No subscription. No ads.
CTA: `Get it on the App Store`

**The problem**
Most dogs who panic at loud noises aren't broken — they just haven't been introduced to the sound on their terms. The existing tools for this are built like they're from 2013, because they are. Loud & Fine does the same proven method (systematic desensitization) without making you read a manual first.

**How it works**
- Pick the sound your dog struggles with — vacuum, storm, fireworks, clippers, crying baby
- Play it at a volume they can handle, reward them, repeat
- The app tracks progress so you know when to turn it up
- No account, no cloud, no data leaving your phone

**Voice sample**
> "[Name] has heard Thunderstorm 5 times and has not once lost their mind. That's growth."

## QUIRKS & ALL — /quirks-and-all

**Hero**
H1: Quirks & All
Tagline: *New sitter. Same quirks.*
Sub: The pet-care handoff doc your sitter actually reads — quirks, commands, and the one thing to know if things go sideways.
CTA: `Get it on the App Store` *(or "Get started free" if that's the actual model — confirm pricing)*

**The problem**
Handing your dog off to a sitter usually means a rushed text, a half-remembered command list, and hoping for the best. Quirks & All turns that into one link: what your dog's actually like, what commands mean what, and a one-tap missing-poster generator you hope you never need.

**How it works**

- If commands drift out of date, the app nudges you to check

## STRAIGHTFORWARD REVIEW — /straightforward-review

*The core idea — most people already know what they want, they just want quick validation — is a genuinely different register from the rest of the family. Loud & Fine and Quirks & All talk to someone anxious and reassure them. Straightforward Review talks to someone decisive and gets out of their way. The copy below leans into that: shorter, flatter, less charm, more utility. It still reads as It's Hypothetical (the deadpan is still there) but it doesn't try to be warm the way the pet apps are — the product's whole value prop is "we won't waste your time," so the copy shouldn't either.*

**Hero**
H1: Straightforward Review
Tagline: *Thumbs up or don't bother.*
Sub: Reviews in two sentences or less. One thumb, up or down. You already know if the place was good — this just makes it quick to say so, and quick to check.
CTA: `[Leave a review / Get notified — TBD on launch status]`

**The problem**
Most reviews are 300 words of someone re-litigating their evening. Nobody reads them, and nobody who writes them enjoys it either. You don't need a novel to know if a place is worth going — you need one honest signal from someone who's already been. Straightforward Review is built around the two things people actually want from a review: was it good, and why, briefly.

**How it works**
- See a place. Give it a thumb — up or down. No stars, no 1-10, no ambiguity.
- Add up to two sentences if you want to. Not required.
- Browsing: see the thumb split first, read the two-liners after — no scrolling through essays to find the one useful line
- That's the whole product. No profiles to build, no "reviewer level," no badges.

**Voice sample**
> "Good. Loud on weekends though."

> "Wouldn't go back. Kitchen closed at 8:45 when the sign says 9."

*(The two-sentence cap should feel like a feature, not a restriction — the copy throughout should reinforce "we removed the parts you didn't want to write" rather than "we're limiting you.")*
